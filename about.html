Prologue · Who I am

Hi, I’m Minh.

I’m from Vietnam. I’m non-binary and trans, and I’m gay. I’m also a senior at Dartmouth, double majoring in Computer Science and Mathematics.

I’m a person of faith, too. I’m Buddhist, and lately I’ve been growing closer to God and to Christianity, through Bible study at Dartmouth’s Tucker Center and going to church. And to stay healthy inside and out, I’ve been doing Pilates and yoga.

Academically, I love AI and machine learning. Professionally, I love finance, business, and trading. Passionately, I love fashion and modeling. And I have a big heart: I love helping people.

So most of what I’ve built has a person on the other end of it: the Deaf community in Vietnam, patients waiting for care, elders and children who need someone watching out for them—and now, fashionistas looking for a space of their own.

My motivation is my mom. You’ll meet her in Chapter Six.

Chapter One · Experience, 2021–2025

Built for people.

Three projects, three communities: a sign-language translator, faster access to care, and cameras that call for help.

96%

96% — Of the 308 Deaf people and people with hearing loss I surveyed in Vietnam, 96% said communicating was difficult. So I built a translator.

Scene 01 · Vietnam · 2021–2023

Signtegrate

My high-school start-up

A real-time Vietnamese Sign Language translator. I collected and annotated 394 sign videos, tracked hands and body with a MediaPipe pipeline, and trained a DD-Net model that recognizes signs in under a second, at 70.1% accuracy.

First runner-up, SV.STARTUP 2022, Vietnam’s national student start-up competition

Made for the Deaf community in Vietnam

Scene 02 · Hanover, NH · 2024

Patient-access research

Dartmouth · FYREE fellow, then E. E. Just summer intern

I cleaned and queried 11,000+ anonymized patient-flow and scheduling records from five Dartmouth-Hitchcock clinics, then built discrete-event simulations in AnyLogic and SimPy to test new scheduling strategies. In simulation, they cut average waits by 25% and raised 10-day patient access from 14–28% to 55%.

Made for patients waiting for care

Scene 03 · Hanoi · 2025

FPT Software

AI Computer Vision Engineer Intern

I worked on a camera-based safety system designed to spot falls—especially for elders and children—and alert firefighters, police, and hospitals. I crawled 10,000+ images across five poses, auto-labeled a 100,000-image dataset (70% less manual labeling), and served a real-time vision-language model on a Jetson Nano with 40% lower latency.

Made for elders, children, and the responders who reach them

Also: I diagnosed a NAT port-mapping issue between a WebRTC client and NVIDIA Omniverse Isaac Sim running in Docker.

Chapter Two · Finance, 2025–2026

Follow the money.

A valuation at Tuck, then a summer in alternative credit at Magnetar Capital.

Scene 04 · Hanover, NH · December 2025

Tuck Business Bridge Program

AECOM valuation · Tuck School of Business

My team built a three-statement model and a DCF for AECOM, projecting $22.6B in revenue and $1.1B in EBIT by 2029: a $109.56 base-case share price, about 11% upside, which we presented to executives and faculty. I led the AI and proprietary-software analysis, turning adoption scenarios into the margin, CapEx, working-capital, and free-cash-flow assumptions.

Made for executives and faculty at Tuck

Scene 05 · Evanston, IL · Summer 2026

Magnetar Capital

Alternative Credit & Fixed Income Summer Analyst

Fundamental credit

I paired qualitative analysis of high-yield issuers with forecast models of their financial statements, testing free cash flow, interest coverage, the maturing debt each would need to refinance, and pro-forma liquidity. That gave me my own view of default risk, which I set against the risk priced into their credit spreads to judge whether each spread was too wide, too tight, or about right. I presented my final recommendation to the firm’s senior investment professionals.

Thematic diligence

I also did diligence on a thematic investment idea. I weighed near-term against long-term demand; assessed the creditworthiness of the companies behind it on liquidity, leverage, execution and regulatory risk, and customer concentration; and mapped the supply chain end to end to find the binding constraint. I then synthesized the findings into an investment recommendation.

Company names, positions, and recommendations from my internship stay confidential. What’s here is how I worked, not what I concluded.

Made for Magnetar’s senior investment team

Chapter Three · What I’m learning

Everyone’s watching AI. I’m watching the power.

AI runs on data centers, and data centers run on power. That’s why I’ve been following nuclear: firm, clean, and, I think, the next big source of power for data centers.

Personal research notes · Built from public sources only · As of September 2026 · Views my own, not any employer’s · Not investment advice

June 23, 2026 The Department of Energy announces up to $17.5B in conditional loans to help build 10 large nuclear reactors Source: energy.gov

  1. 01

    The demand

    AI is spreading into every industry: LLMs today, vision-language models and robotics next. Hyperscalers are spending billions on the data centers to run it all, and those need an enormous amount of power, around the clock.

  2. 02

    Firm and clean

    Renewables are intermittent, and the 2026 Middle East crisis has left global gas markets tight and volatile. Nuclear runs day and night, without the carbon.

  3. 03

    The funding gap

    Many next-generation reactor developers are pre-revenue with no credit ratings, burning cash on units targeted for around 2030 to the early 2030s. For now, their cash flow hinges on government support and early commitments from big power buyers.

  4. 04

    The catalysts

    In June, the DOE announced up to $17.5B in conditional loans to help build 10 large AP1000 reactors: Washington putting its balance sheet behind new nuclear. Amazon, Google, and Meta have signed nuclear deals too, from equity stakes to prepayments.

  5. 05

    Who benefits

    Many of those deals are staged or early-stage, so the sooner reactors get built, the sooner that money turns into revenue. For lenders and investors across the sector, news like this signals potential mega deals ahead.

  6. 06

    The bottleneck I’m watching

    Many advanced reactor designs call for HALEU: uranium enriched to between 5% and just under 20%. My read of the public record is that the choke point is enrichment—how fast the US can add the centrifuge capacity to make HALEU at scale.

What would change my mind: new reactors slipping well past their early-2030s targets, big tech’s power deals staying non-binding, or new enrichment plants coming online after the reactors that need their fuel.

Sources: DOE loan announcement · IEA Gas Market Report, Q3 2026 · DOE: What is HALEU?

Chapter Six · Motivation

The closet she opened for me.

My mom is hard-working, ambitious, and so caring. In her twenties, she was the CFO of a tech start-up, before she gave her career up for our family.

The day before my flight back to college, she took me to a closet she had kept tucked away for decades. It was full of her clothes from her own college years in the 2000s. She had great fashion sense. She told me to pick whatever I wanted for college.

I was so emotional. Ever since coming out as non-binary and trans, it has felt ten times harder to navigate life and acceptance back home in Vietnam, where many people still hold traditional, conservative views.

I know I’ll always have her beside me, and I know how much she has sacrificed for me to be who I am today. That’s what drives me—in AI, in finance, and on the runway. Someday, I’m going to take her to places she’s never been, so she never has to save up for a vacation again.
